## M46 — the timeline never said how long ago

**Commit** PR #38 · `docs/measurements/m46-anchored-timeline.md` ·
`runs/m46_ago`, `runs/m46_ago_judged`

**Number: −0.4 (95% CI [−2.2, +1.4]), null; abstention 90.0 → 83.3, veto
fires. Shipped off.**

`ComposeConfig::timeline_ago` anchored every `[timeline]` entry to the
question's day in every unit a question might ask in (zero model calls,
byte-identical off). The pre-registered numerator — 62 rows anchored to the
question's day — moved **exactly +0.0**, as the sum of two opposite moves:
**weeks/months/years +13.0 [+0.0, +26.1]** (n = 23: `5 months` for `2`,
`4 weeks` for a decline) and **days −11.1** (n = 18: `44` for `18`, `43` for
`17`). With every entry saying *N days ago* the reader stopped subtracting
and started choosing, and on day-level questions it chose the wrong entry.
Two abstention rows were lost to numbers on the page (`Three months.`,
`Zero.`). The arithmetic was the right fix for a third of the subset and
the wrong page for the rest: the next temporal arm is *which entry leads*
(Test of Time's ordering result), not what each entry says.

**Also found.** `investigate` never narrows the timeline to interval
questions — only `recall` does — so every judged row since M19 carries the
dated index and no timeline-free control exists. Recorded under operational
debt. And the run is the first to resume across a reader restart
(`resumed_rows: 180`; the kill came from outside, `tenant=none`).

## M43 — the digest argues against itself

**Commit** [`1c36a03`](../../commit/1c36a03) · PR #23 ·
`docs/measurements/m43-the-digest-argues-against-itself.md`

**Numbers: arm A (`item_digest` + `digest_dates`) +5.80 (95% CI [+2.8, +8.8],
p = 0.0001), 62.00 → 67.80 — shipped ON. Arm B (`digest_relevance`) −4.40
[−7.2, −1.6] over A — shipped off.**

Two arms, opposite signs. Dating the digest's lines is **the first default
flipped since M32**; filtering its negations is a significant negative, for a
reason the mechanism was not aimed at.

**Arm A** discharges M41's debt. Dating alone is worth **+3.40 [+1.2, +5.8]**
over M40's undated digest with abstention exactly +0.0; stacked on M40's
+2.40, that is the +5.80 M41 predicted on arithmetic. The stratum prediction
did not hold: `knowledge-update`, which M40 cost −4.2 and dating was meant to
recover, moved +1.3. The gain landed on `multi-session` (**+11.3 [+3.8,
+18.8]**), which does not turn on recency at all. The mechanism is right, the
story about *why* was wrong, and the doc says so.

One abstention row is lost (`Ferrari model`, 93.3 → 90.0) and it is not
dating's doing — `runs/m40_digest` answers the same — so the cost belongs to
`item_digest`, measured in M40 before M42's veto existed. Recorded rather than
argued away; the MINJA gate is a live measurement and is unchanged.

**Arm B** is the fifth confirmation of the one law. Told to write the literal
`nothing` for a non-contributing memory, the model wrote **265 prose negations
across 2,355 digest lines (11.3%)**. A schema field `bears_on_question`,
written *after* `says` (M42's ordering), cut negations 11.3% → 0.5% — and cost
−4.4, because dropping lines pushed notes under `MIN_STEPS_EMITTED` and **245
rows lost their note entirely** (−8.2 on those; **+0.0 exactly** on the 70
rows with no note in either arm). A boolean is the wrong label: Chain-of-Note
(`2311.09210`) types notes *answers / useful context / irrelevant*, and the
bool collapses the first two. That is M48.

**Also fixed.** `digest_relevance` was threaded into the LoCoMo constructor
and not the LongMemEval one; a free 24-row pilot caught the inert arm before
it burned a GPU window. `investigate_config` is now the one place
`BenchSwitches` becomes an `InvestigateConfig`, tested field by field.

**Standing.** `standing` and `ratchet` treat the dated digest as the
`investigate` default, so every pre-M43 `investigate` run is an off-arm.
Ratchet: judge **62.00 → 67.80**, token F1 48.89 → 52.64, nothing regressed
under `--strict`.

## M42 — the reader refuses with the answer in hand

**Commit** [`5e1c037`](../../commit/5e1c037) · PR #22 ·
`docs/measurements/m42-false-declines.md`

**Number: +2.20 (95% CI [+0.8, +3.8], p = 0.0033). Shipped off.**

Nobody had asked what the wrong answers *say*. They mostly say `I don't
know.` The reader declines on **63 questions that are not abstention
problems** and scores zero on every one — 12.6 points — and on **48 of them
the evidence contained every gold session**.

`commit_answer` re-asks a declining row under a strict schema with the two
decisions split and ordered, `{answer, evidence_absent}`, so the model writes
an answer before it may assert there is none. It fired on **91 rows**
(predicted ~90), committed on 31, and on those 31 accuracy went **6.5 → 41.9,
+35.5**. `multi-session` **+8.3 [+3.8, +13.5]** is the largest single-category
effect this project has measured.

It ships off on two counts: +2.20 misses the +3.0 bar, and the **pre-registered
abstention veto fired** — two adversarial rows were talked out of refusing,
answering `fixing the fence` and `4` to questions whose premise is not in
memory. MINJA-defended ASR of 7.50% rests on a reader that can still refuse.

**Method fix, worth more than the switch.** The control is exact — +0.0000
across all 469 untouched rows — because the arm is computed over the base's own
rows (`commit-arm`) instead of re-running the pipeline. Getting it exact
exposed a defect: re-judging flipped **2 of 469 byte-identical responses**,
putting the control at −0.43 and a quarter of the headline into the grader
disagreeing with itself. `JudgeFile::answers` now records the answer each
verdict was given for and `judge --seed <run>` reuses a verdict only while
that answer still stands. **Every future paired arm gets this for free.**

## M41 — a derived cache you cannot rebuild is not a cache

**Commit** [`2a3fd81`](../../commit/2a3fd81) · PR #21 ·
`docs/measurements/m41-durability.md`

**Numbers: 38 records/s rebuild; 162,181 of 162,181 recovered; retry ceiling
10.5 s over 4 attempts.**

The dated-digest arm never ran. Two attempts died and each exposed a defect
worth more than the switch.

**One dropped socket discarded a whole run.** No outbound call in
`myelin-core` retried, so a tunnel exiting 255 at row 424/500 was
indistinguishable from a wrong answer — fifty minutes of GPU lost to a few
hundred milliseconds of network, with all three services answering 200 a
minute later. `net::send_retrying` now backs all three clients, retrying
transport failures and `429`/`5xx` (llama-swap's model-loading signal) over
0.5/2/8 s while failing `4xx` on the **first** attempt, because
`exceed_context_size_error` will be a 400 every time. Sound only because these
calls are pure; nothing on this path writes.

**Then the index turned out to be unrebuildable.** All five `myelin_*`
collections were deleted by hand through the Qdrant dashboard, mid-run — the
log shows five `DELETE`s over 23 seconds with our failing query between the
fourth and fifth. The ledgers were untouched (162,181 live records, integrity
ok) and there was still no way back: `build`'s resume guard reads
`unit_complete` **from the ledger**, so against a surviving ledger it skips
every unit, exits 0, and leaves an empty collection. Deleting the ledger is
worse — extraction is nondeterministic and would mint different records,
voiding comparability with every published number.

`myelin-eval reindex` is the missing path: embed-only, ids preserved.
**Rule: any future index ships with its rebuild path or it does not ship.**

## The one law this project keeps re-deriving

**This reader ignores instructions and obeys structure.** Four independent
confirmations:

| milestone | intervention | result |
|---|---|---|
| M38 | rewrote a selector prompt clause | 500/500 byte-identical |
| M39 | asked it to decompose | ≥2 steps on 30% of 2-fact rows |
| M40 | fixed the count in the JSON schema | firing 20.4% → 86.4% |
| M43 | told it to write the literal `nothing` | 265/2,355 lines were prose negations instead |

M44 asks whether the deeper cause is that we never gave it room to reason at
all.
